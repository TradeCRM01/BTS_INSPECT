## 2. What this policy covers

This policy covers personal information we collect and hold when:

- you create a Grafter workspace or are invited into one
- you use Grafter to run jobs, quotes, invoices, timesheets, inspections, JHAs / SWMS / Take 5, stock, and related field paperwork
- you upload photos, receipt scans, or other files
- you send or receive email or SMS through Grafter
- someone visits our public pages (home, sign-in, create a workspace)

It covers:

- **Account holders and crew** — you and your team
- **People you store in Grafter** — clients, contacts, suppliers, and anyone who appears in job records, photos, or paperwork

If you are a contractor using Grafter, you may also have your own privacy obligations to your clients and crew. You are responsible for how you collect and use *their* information. See section 12.

## 3. What personal information we collect

### About you and your crew

When you create a workspace we collect:

- your name
- company / business name
- email address
- password (stored as a hash — we cannot read your password)

Depending on how you use Grafter, we may also hold:

- phone number, role, and profile details
- company details you enter (address, ABN, licence numbers, logo, payment instructions)
- team invites (name, email, role)
- timesheets and labour records
- support messages you send us
- sign-in and security logs (for example, that an account signed in)

### About clients, suppliers, and job sites

You (or your team) may enter:

- client and contact names, emails, phone numbers, and addresses
- job-site addresses and job notes
- quotes, invoices, purchase orders, and payment status
- supplier contacts
- inspection, JHA / SWMS, and Take 5 records, including crew names and signatures
- photos from site — these may include people who are on site
- receipt and invoice scans (vendor name, amounts, dates, and anything else visible on the document)
- emails and SMS you send to clients or crew through Grafter (job reminders, quotes, invoices, and similar)

We do not go looking for extra personal information about your clients. We hold what you put in Grafter, plus what is needed to deliver the messages you ask us to send.

### Sensitive information

Some field paperwork (for example a JHA, incident note, or a photo of an injury) can include health or other sensitive information. We only collect this if you or your crew enter or upload it, so we can provide the service. We do not use it for marketing.

### What we do not collect on public pages

We do not currently run advertising pixels or third-party analytics on Grafter’s public pages. Signing in uses a session stored in your browser so you stay logged in.

## 4. How we collect it

**Directly from you**

- Creating a workspace (name, company name, email, password)
- Signing in, resetting a password, or accepting a team invite
- Entering jobs, clients, timesheets, invoices, JHAs, and other records
- Uploading photos, PDFs, and receipt scans
- Connecting optional tools (for example Xero) from Settings
- Contacting us for support

**Signup does not confirm your email.** When you create a workspace we do **not** send a “please confirm this email” message. The account is created so you can sign in straight away. That means a workspace can be created with an email address the person does not control. We only send email for later flows such as password reset and team invitations. If an account was opened with your email and it was not you, contact us and we will help sort it out.

**From your organisation**

- An admin can invite crew by email. The invite email is sent to the address they enter.
- Team members can add or update client, job, and crew records.

**From other people, when you ask us to message them**

- If you send a quote, invoice, job reminder, or portal link, we use the contact details you stored so we can deliver that message.

**Automatically**

- Technical logs needed to run and secure the service (for example, that a sign-in succeeded or failed). We do not use these for advertising.

We generally cannot offer Grafter anonymously. It is an account-based business tool. You need a name, company name, and email to open a workspace.

## 5. Why we collect it

We collect, hold, use, and disclose personal information to:

- provide Grafter — quotes, jobs, scheduling, timesheets, invoices, inspections, JHAs / SWMS / Take 5, stock, and related records
- create and administer your workspace and team access
- send service messages you ask for (password reset, team invites, job reminders, quotes, invoices)
- provide optional features you turn on (SMS reminders, Xero, in-app AI / receipt scan)
- give support and fix faults
- keep the service secure and prevent misuse
- meet legal obligations (for example tax or a lawful request)

We do not sell personal information.

We do not use your client list to market other companies’ products.

## 6. Who we disclose it to

We disclose personal information only as needed to run Grafter, as you instruct, or as the law requires.

**Your own organisation**

People in your workspace can see records their role allows — jobs, clients, timesheets, photos, and so on.

**Your clients and crew, when you send them something**

If you send a quote, invoice, reminder, or a client-portal link, the recipient sees what you chose to send.

**Service providers (subprocessors)**

We use other companies to host and send things. They only get what they need to do that job:

| Who | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| **Supabase** | Database, file storage, authentication, and serverless functions |
| **Cloudflare** | Hosts the Grafter web app |
| **Resend and/or Supabase email** | Sends password-reset, invite, quote, invoice, and similar emails |
| **Twilio** | Sends SMS if you use SMS job reminders |
| **Stripe** | Processes subscription payments if you are billed |
| **Xero** | Accounting sync, only if you connect it |
| **Anthropic** | Optional AI assistant and receipt scanning, only if those features are used |
| **Tavily** | Optional web search from the AI console, only if that tool is used |

We may also disclose information to professional advisers, or if the law, a court, or a regulator requires it, or if we sell or restructure the business (the new operator would have to handle information in line with this policy).

We do not sell personal information to data brokers.

## 7. Overseas disclosure (APP 8)

Grafter is built in Australia. Some of our service providers process information **outside Australia**.

In particular:

- **Supabase**, **Cloudflare**, **Resend**, **Stripe**, **Twilio**, and **Anthropic** commonly process information in the **United States** and in other countries where they operate.
- **Xero** (if you connect it) may process information in New Zealand, Australia, and other countries where Xero operates.

We cannot always name every country in advance because providers operate regional networks. If it is practicable to be more specific, we will update this policy.

Before we disclose personal information to an overseas recipient, we take reasonable steps to ensure they handle it in a way that is consistent with the APPs. We remain responsible for personal information we disclose overseas, as the Privacy Act requires.

By creating an account or using Grafter, you understand that personal information may be stored or processed outside Australia as described above.

## 8. Storage and security (APP 11)

We take reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, interference, loss, and unauthorised access, modification, or disclosure.

In practice that includes:

- access controls so people in a workspace only see their organisation’s records
- hashed passwords
- hosting with established cloud providers
- limiting staff access to what is needed to support the service

No internet service is perfectly secure. You also have a part to play: use a strong password, keep it to yourself, and be careful who you invite into the workspace.

We do not claim SOC 2, ISO 27001, or a specific encryption standard in this policy. If we publish a security page later, it will match what we actually do.

When we no longer need personal information for the purpose we collected it, and we are not required by law to keep it, we take reasonable steps to destroy it or de-identify it.

## 9. Access and correction (APP 12 and APP 13)

You can ask us for access to personal information we hold about you, and ask us to correct it if it is wrong, out of date, incomplete, or misleading.

**If you have a Grafter account**, start in the app (profile, company, and the records you can edit). That is the fastest way.

**Anyone** (including a client whose details a contractor stored) can email privacy@grafter.com.au or call 0486 011 187. Tell us who you are and what you want to see or fix. We may need to verify your identity.

We will respond within a reasonable time. If we refuse access or correction in a way the Privacy Act allows, we will tell you why (unless we are not allowed to) and how to complain.

We generally do not charge for access. If a request would cost a lot to meet, we will talk to you first.

If the information sits in a contractor’s workspace and they are the right organisation to deal with, we may need to work with them as well.

## 10. Retention and deletion

We keep personal information while your workspace is open and we need it to provide Grafter.

If you close your account or ask us to delete a workspace, we will delete or de-identify personal information when we no longer need it, unless we must keep it for:

- a legal obligation (for example tax records)
- a dispute or claim
- security logs for a limited period

Some records you store (invoices, JHAs, timesheets) are the kind of thing a trade business keeps for years. If you need a copy before you close the workspace, export or download what you need first, or ask us for help.

Deletion is not always instant — backups expire on a cycle — but we do not keep using closed-workspace data to provide the service.

## 11. Direct marketing (APP 7)

We do not sell your information for other people’s marketing.

We may email you about **the service itself**: password resets, invites, billing (if we charge you), security notices, and changes to this policy or the Terms of Use.

We do not currently run a separate marketing newsletter. If we ever do, we will only do so as the law allows, and you will be able to opt out.

Service emails that you need to use the product (reset, invite, invoice you asked us to send) are not “marketing”.

## 12. Your responsibilities when you store other people’s information

Grafter holds whatever you put in it. That often includes personal information about your clients, crew, and people on site.

If you are an APP entity (or you otherwise have privacy duties), you must:

- only put information in Grafter that you are allowed to collect
- tell those people how you handle their information, where the law requires a notice
- not use Grafter to send spam or messages people have not agreed to receive
- be careful with photos — a site photo can identify a person

We process that information to provide Grafter to you. We do not use your client list for our own marketing.

## 13. Optional AI features

Some Grafter features can send content to an AI provider (currently Anthropic) so the product can, for example:

- answer in-app questions
- read a receipt scan and suggest expense fields

Those features only run if they are set up and you use them. The content you submit (which may include personal information on a receipt or in a prompt) may be processed **outside Australia**.

These tools help you do a job in the app. They do not decide whether a person gets credit, insurance, a job, or a government benefit.

## 16. Notifiable data breaches

We take seriously any unauthorised access to, disclosure of, or loss of personal information.

If we have reasonable grounds to believe there is an **eligible data breach** under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme (a breach likely to result in serious harm, that we cannot prevent with prompt action), we will:

- notify the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC), and
- notify affected individuals,

as the Privacy Act requires, including who we are, what happened, the kinds of information involved, and recommended steps.
